# Ara schematic, revision A prototype

This is the editable circuit for five or ten Arduino-compatible prototype boards. A routed `ara.kicad_pcb` and its `ara.kicad_pro` project now sit beside the schematic. The [quotation package](../manufacturing/rev-a/README.md) contains the board files, assembly files and firmware. Physical operation remains untested.

## Files

- `ara.kicad_sch`: self-contained KiCad 6-or-later schematic with embedded symbols and explicit physical pin numbers.
- `ara.svg`: zoomable review drawing of the same component and net definitions.
- `ara.net`: connectivity XML for review, generated by this project's script rather than by KiCad.
- `circuit.json`: component values, selected manufacturer part numbers, footprints and pin connections. The factory BOM is in `../manufacturing/rev-a/bom.csv`.
- `generate.py`: regenerates those four files using Python's standard library.
- `check.py`: reconstructs connections from the native schematic's symbols, wires and labels and checks the design requirements.
- `checks.json`: results of the most recent connectivity and calculation checks.
- `SOURCES.md`: datasheets, pin-map references and sourcing limitations.

Open `ara.kicad_pro` in KiCad to edit the schematic and PCB together. The drawing has six sections on a custom sheet that leaves the title block clear of components. Labels with the same name connect electrically even when there is no drawn wire between sections. The schematic-folder SVG is a separate rendering from the shared circuit model. The manufacturing package also includes KiCad's native SVG export, which was rendered and inspected.

## Circuit choices

The ATMEGA328P-AU is the surface-mount version of the processor in the working Uno. Its 32-pin package has different pin numbers from the existing 28-pin chip. The schematic uses TQFP pin 13, PB1/OC1A, for Arduino digital pin 9. VCC is 5 V and the three-pin resonator is 16 MHz. Both match the installed Arduino AVR Uno build configuration and the current `ara_birdsong` sketch. The CSTNE16M0V530000R0 resonator replaces the earlier CSTCE candidate and includes its 15 pF load capacitors. Murata publishes a comparison drawing for the two parts.

The USB-C receptacle has separate 5.1k pull-down resistors on CC1 and CC2. Both orientations' USB data contacts are connected to the CH340C USB-to-serial chip. The data lines have a USBLC6-2SC6 ESD protection array. A resettable fuse and TPS22918 load switch precede the board's power rail. The load switch slows the charging of the downstream capacitors. It is not a current regulator, a USB-PD controller or reverse-current protection.

The CH340C runs from 5 V. Its V3 pin has its own 100nF capacitor and is not tied to 5 V. Its TXD feeds the AVR's RXD through R4, and the AVR's TXD feeds the CH340C RXD through R5. A falling DTR edge resets the AVR through C10. D1 clamps the corresponding positive reset pulse. J2 provides conventional six-pin AVR ISP access for initial programming and recovery. U2 pin 8 is deliberately unused; it must not be treated as a crystal pin on CH340C.

The audio path takes the existing PWM output through two RC stages, a fixed attenuation network and a DC-blocking capacitor. The PAM8302A is an analogue-input amplifier, so it does not require a firmware port to I2S. The 100k/1k attenuation is a conservative starting point for quiet birdsong. It must be adjusted against a physical listening test. The linear calculation includes filter loading, the coupling capacitor, and a nominal 10k amplifier input resistance. It predicts about -18 dB net voltage gain from PWM baseband to the speaker at 1 kHz, including the amplifier's nominal gain of 15. That is a calculation, not a measured volume or power specification. The filter also attenuates the highest bird notes more than lower notes; compare all phrases before selecting final values.

The amplifier outputs are bridged. J3 connects between its two outputs through ferrite beads. Neither speaker lead connects to ground. C18/C19 provide high-frequency shunting after those beads. The speaker pair should be kept short and twisted, with enclosure strain relief.

R10/C17 provide a startup delay at the amplifier enable pin. Arduino digital pin 4 can pull the enable low through R11 if a later firmware change adds muting. The current sketch leaves that pin as an input, so the amplifier is enabled by the pull-up after the RC delay. The hardware delay does not wait for the bootloader to finish; startup/shutdown clicks and USB-reset clicks need testing. No firmware was changed for this draft.

## Power and programming

Use an external regulated 5 V USB supply for normal operation. An available 500mA-or-higher rating is a proposed supply choice, not the board's measured consumption or permission to draw that much from any USB host. The quiet attenuation is intended to keep demand modest. Measure startup, idle and audio current before release, including comparison with the CH340C's USB descriptors and the host's allowed current. USB suspend behaviour also needs review. An Rd resistor does not by itself authorize 1.5 A or 3 A of draw.

For the first prototypes, power the target through USB when using J2 and disable the programmer's target-power output. J2 pin 2 is a 5 V reference connection, not a second independent power input. This draft does not provide power-source arbitration.

Blank ATmega chips need initial ISP programming. The installed Arduino AVR 1.8.6 Uno configuration uses Optiboot, a 16 MHz clock, 115200-baud serial uploads and fuse bytes low `0xFF`, high `0xDE`, extended `0xFD`. Use the toolchain's documented bootloader-burning procedure and verify the exact purchased chip before setting fuses. Clock or fuse mistakes can prevent normal uploads. The factory must not substitute ATmega328PB or an unverified compatible device without a separate firmware and pinout review.

## Checks completed

Regenerate and check with:

```sh
python3 hardware/schematic/generate.py
python3 hardware/schematic/check.py
```

The checker reconstructs pin connections from the actual `.kicad_sch` geometry, verifies the independent `.net` agrees, checks USB orientation and CC wiring, AVR pin assignments, UART direction, reset diode polarity, recovery header order, supply connections, filter topology and isolated speaker outputs. Every pin has a connection or an intentional no-connect marker. KiUtils 1.4.8 also parsed the native schematic and round-tripped it successfully during authoring. The SVG was rendered and visually inspected with CairoSVG.

KiCad 10.0.8 was run from a verified official disk image on 25 November 2026. Native ERC reports zero findings. Native PCB DRC reports zero findings, zero unconnected items and zero schematic parity issues. The [reports](../manufacturing/rev-a/checks/) are retained with the manufacturing files. Native SVGs of the schematic and board were rendered and inspected. No circuit simulation or physical measurement has run.

Exact procurement parts are recorded in `../pcb/parts.json` and a dated public JLCPCB snapshot in `../pcb/sourcing.json`. The selected capacitors now use stocked X7R parts for small values and X5R parts for bulk supply capacitance. Their actual voltage ratings are recorded in the circuit. The ideal filter calculation does not model tolerance, DC bias or temperature. The ferrite beads are BLM21PG221SZ1D. The Alps SKRPACE010 reset switch has a custom footprint derived from its manufacturer's land pattern and contact diagram.

## Prototype quotation and ordering

1. Match all 27 part numbers in the factory quotation and inspect the corrected placement preview. Supplier rotation/origin corrections are retained separately from the KiCad layout. The dated stock snapshot is not a reservation. Do not substitute ATmega328PB for ATmega328P.
2. Include through-hole soldering for the speaker connector and programming header. Specify initial bootloader, fuse and firmware programming separately. Blank chips cannot accept USB serial uploads until their initial programming is complete.
3. Compare the five- and ten-board delivered prices. An independent review is an optional risk-reduction step before buying the prototype batch, as agreed with the owner.
4. Test the amplifier with the actual speaker after delivery. Compare quiet volume, high notes, silence and startup clicks with the working Uno. Supply current, USB behaviour and physical audio performance remain unverified.
5. Build the enclosure around the board and speaker. The board is 65 × 45 × 1.6 mm, excluding components and connector access. Four 3.2 mm mounting holes have 6.4 mm copper-free screw-head areas. The STEP file is the bare board, not an assembled clearance model.

The final speaker cable must arrive pre-fitted with the mating JST-PH plug. The owner should not need to solder or crimp it. Fit and retention remain physical tests for the assembled enclosure.
